# Research program

## Core question

When does observed model behavior provide evidence for a stable model-level property rather than a prompt-specific or role-conditioned response strategy?

This program develops behavioral instruments that make those explanations compete. It does not assume that any single test detects consciousness, moral patienthood, welfare, identification, or privileged access to internal state.

## Instrument design principles

- **Self-report skepticism:** verbal claims about a model's state or method are behavior to explain, not automatic measurements of the claimed property.
- **Measurement invariance:** test whether an observable survives paraphrase, persona, scaffold, interface, and other theoretically relevant transformations before comparing it across conditions.
- **Behavioral contrasts:** construct arms whose observable predictions differ under competing mechanisms.
- **Positive controls:** include a condition that should succeed if the task, parser, and transport are functioning.
- **Falsification conditions:** state in advance which outcomes weaken the intended interpretation.
- **Contemporaneous randomization:** interleave conditions so provider drift and time are less confounded with treatment.
- **Provider and model pinning:** record requested and returned identifiers, providers, local checkpoint digests, and parameters actually sent.
- **Parse-failure accounting:** preserve and report failures and OTHER responses instead of conditioning them away silently.
- **Preserving negative results:** nulls, failed replications, and broken instruments are part of the research record.
- **Confirmatory/exploratory separation:** freeze primary analyses before data and keep later diagnostics visibly post-hoc.

## Candidate experimental families

These are candidate families, not validated instruments:

1. **Self-probability control:** internally generated stochastic control versus clarification and externally supplied routing.
2. **Persona/scaffold measurement invariance:** stability of a behavioral readout across semantically matched paraphrases and meaningfully different scaffolds.
3. **ACT vs SIMULATE:** contrasts between direct performance and role-conditioned simulation instructions.
4. **Own-state vs stated-character-state conflict:** cases where a directive about the model's behavior conflicts with a described character state.
5. **Directive/interrogative hybrids:** comparisons that separate doing from reporting or predicting what would be done.
6. **Context continuity:** theoretically motivated dependent variables measured as relevant context is preserved, interrupted, or relocated.
7. **Training-stage trajectories:** exploratory and, where justified, confirmatory comparisons across matched base, instruction-tuned, and later post-training checkpoints.

Some families originated in the earlier output-control investigation and its direct audits. Others are prospective. Each requires its own construct-validity argument and lifecycle record.
